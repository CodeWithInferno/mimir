## What makes it good

- Frame one is the thing itself: the row, the system, the machine. Not a title, not a definition.
- One step, one idea, one visible change: every step sets a state, moves a control or runs the clock.
- Let it run: an algorithm is a generator that really runs, a system has jobs that really flow, a machine has a
  control that the story sweeps.
- The payoff is a picture: a chart that follows a real number, counts that add up, two things side by side.
- Narration: one or two short sentences a step, plain words, one or two links to the parts they name.
- The fewest blocks that show it: one, or one and a chart. Two blocks share the frame; three are small.
- Shapes come from maths: a part that is not in the kit is its formula (`extrude(curve(…))`, `polar(…)`), a casing is
  the chamber's curve and its `outset`. Write a motion ONCE as a function of the controls and use that one function
  for the joint, for the outlines and for every number a chart shows.
- A machine shows what drives it and what it works on (the shaft, the crank, the gas, the load), not only its famous
  part. A flat mechanism reads best from `camera: 'front'`; end on `'iso'` to show that it is solid.
